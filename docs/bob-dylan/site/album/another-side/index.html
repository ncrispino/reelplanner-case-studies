Greenwich Village · 1961–1964

Listen to the album

Why it matters

Recorded in a single night session in June 1964, it trades topical protest for personal, playful and searching songs. It marked his break from the role of folk spokesman.

Dylan cut this album in New York in one long evening in June 1964, with Tom Wilson producing and friends drifting in and out of the studio. He played it almost entirely alone on guitar and harmonica, with piano on one track, and the loose, sometimes giggling performances reflect the speed of the session. Many of the songs had been written on travels earlier that year, including a stay in Europe. Gone were the newspaper-headline ballads; in their place came comic shaggy-dog stories, flirtatious love songs, a bitter account of a breakup and a few searching reflections on his own earlier certainties. Folk purists were dismayed, and parts of the folk press publicly scolded him for turning inward. Other artists heard something new, and the Byrds and the Turtles would soon find hits in its songs. Looking back, it is the hinge between the protest singer and the electric poet who arrived next.
