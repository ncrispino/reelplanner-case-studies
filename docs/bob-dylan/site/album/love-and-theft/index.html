"Love and Theft"

Late Renaissance · 1997–2012

Listen to the album

Why it matters

Released on September 11, 2001, it set Dylan's songs inside the sounds of prewar America: blues, rockabilly, swing and Tin Pan Alley. He produced it himself under the name Jack Frost with his touring band, and its title in quotation marks nods to a scholarly book on blackface minstrelsy.

Dylan recorded "Love and Theft" in New York in 2001 with his touring band of the time, including guitarists Larry Campbell and Charlie Sexton, bassist Tony Garnier and drummer David Kemper, along with Augie Meyers on keyboards. He produced it himself under the name Jack Frost, and the sessions were quick and relaxed. The music moves freely through older American styles: jump blues, rockabilly, swing, parlor ballads and crooner numbers, played with the ease of a band that knew him well. Dylan sings with wit and menace, and the songs are packed with borrowed lines and characters from old records, films and books, a habit that drew both admiration and debate. "Mississippi", left off Time Out of Mind, appears here in a warm new version. The album came out on the day of the September 11 attacks, yet critics embraced it, and it won the Grammy for Best Contemporary Folk Album.
