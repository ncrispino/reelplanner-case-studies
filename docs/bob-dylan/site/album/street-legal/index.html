Blood on the Tracks · 1973–1978

Listen to the album

Why it matters

Recorded in his own Santa Monica studio with the large band from his 1978 tour, it pairs dense, symbolic songs with horns and gospel-style backing singers.

After a world tour in early 1978 with a large band, Dylan took the same group into Rundown, his rehearsal studio in Santa Monica, and recorded the album in about a week with producer Don DeVito. The sound is big and soulful: saxophone, keyboards and a trio of gospel-trained women singers answering his vocals, a long way from the folk or country records of the past. The songs are dense and full of symbolic imagery, about broken relationships, spiritual searching and a sense of danger, and they foreshadow the religious turn that came soon after. The original mix was widely criticized as murky, and American critics were generally cool. In Britain it found a warmer welcome, helped by a big European tour that year. A remixed version issued in 1999 brought out the detail, and the album's reputation has risen steadily since.
