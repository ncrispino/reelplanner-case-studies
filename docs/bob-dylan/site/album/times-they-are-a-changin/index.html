The Times They Are a-Changin'

Greenwich Village · 1961–1964

Listen to the album

Why it matters

His first album of entirely original songs is his most starkly topical, with ballads of poverty, racist murder and unjust courts. Its title song became an anthem of the 1960s, and its closing farewell already hints that he was ready to move on.

Recorded in New York in the late summer and autumn of 1963 with Tom Wilson producing, his third album is the most austere record he ever made: one voice, one guitar, a harmonica, and nothing to soften the subject matter. These are songs of the civil rights era and of hard times in small towns, several drawn from real events, including the killing of Medgar Evers and the death of Hattie Carroll in Baltimore. Alongside them sit a few personal songs about distance and parting that hint at a different writer underneath. The title track was written to be an anthem and was taken up as one almost immediately. The record arrived in early 1964, just weeks after the assassination of John F. Kennedy, and it cemented his place as the voice of protest. That was a role he was already growing restless in, and within months he would set it aside.
