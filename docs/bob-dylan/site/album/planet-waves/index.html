Blood on the Tracks · 1973–1978

Listen to the album

Why it matters

Recorded with the Band in Los Angeles, it was his first album for Asylum and his first to reach number one in the US.

Dylan had not made a full album of new songs in more than three years when he went into a Los Angeles studio in November 1973 with the Band, his old companions from 1966 and the basement sessions. The record was made fast, in a few days, with engineer Rob Fraboni, and the Band's playing is loose, rootsy and warm, built on Garth Hudson's organ and Robbie Robertson's guitar. The songs are mostly about home, love and devotion, with a few darker ones running underneath, and "Forever Young" appears in two contrasting versions, slow and fast. Dylan drew the cover art himself. Released in January 1974 on Asylum, it coincided with his first major tour in eight years, with the Band, which drew enormous demand for tickets. Critics were respectful rather than overwhelmed, and the album has tended to be overshadowed by the one that followed.
