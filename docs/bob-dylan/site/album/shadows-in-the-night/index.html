Shadows in the Night

The Standards · 2015–2017

Listen to the album

Why it matters

Dylan's first album of standards strips songs Frank Sinatra once recorded down to his small touring band, recorded live in the studio.

For Shadows in the Night, Dylan took ten songs that Frank Sinatra had recorded and cut them at Capitol Studios in Hollywood, where Sinatra had made many of his records. Rather than copy the big orchestral arrangements, he scaled them down for his own five-piece band, with Donnie Herron's pedal steel carrying the lines once played by strings, and a few horns on some tracks. The musicians played together live around a small number of microphones, with engineer Al Schmitt capturing them with little or no overdubbing. The result is hushed and slow, and Dylan sings with surprising care and steadiness, staying close to the melodies. He said he saw the project not as covering Sinatra but as uncovering the songs. Many critics were moved by it, and he gave away copies to readers of a magazine for older Americans. It began the run of standards albums that filled the next three years.
