Under the Red Sky

The '80s · 1983–1990

Listen to the album

Why it matters

Produced by Don Was and David Was with guests including George Harrison, Elton John and Slash, it mixes nursery-rhyme simplicity with a rock band sound.

Dylan made Under the Red Sky quickly in Los Angeles in early 1990 with the brothers Don and David Was producing, the same year he recorded with the Traveling Wilburys. A long list of famous players dropped in, among them George Harrison, Elton John, Slash, David Crosby, Bruce Hornsby and the brothers Stevie Ray and Jimmie Vaughan. The music is straightforward rock and blues, often built on short, simple riffs, and many of the songs are written like nursery rhymes or fables, with riddling characters and plain, repeating forms. The album is dedicated to a young child, which some listeners take as a key to its tone. Coming after Oh Mercy, it puzzled critics, and the opening "Wiggle Wiggle" was widely mocked. Over time more listeners have come to hear the fairy-tale songs, and the title track in particular, as deliberate and even haunting rather than careless.
