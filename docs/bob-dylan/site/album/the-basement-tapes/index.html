Blood on the Tracks · 1973–1978

Listen to the album

Why it matters

Columbia finally released these 1967 recordings made with the Band near Woodstock in 1975, adding some Band-only tracks to the Dylan songs that had circulated for years on bootlegs.

In 1967, while Dylan recovered from his accident near Woodstock, he and the Hawks gathered almost daily to play, first at his home and then in the basement of a pink house in West Saugerties that the group shared. Garth Hudson recorded the sessions on a simple tape machine. They ran through old folk songs, country tunes and dozens of new Dylan songs, some funny and nonsensical, others haunting. Demo tapes went out to other artists, and hits followed for Manfred Mann, the Byrds and others, while bootlegs spread the music from 1969 on. When Columbia finally issued this double album in 1975, Robbie Robertson compiled it, adding tracks by the Band alone and some later overdubs. Critics welcomed it warmly, and its sound, rough, homemade and rooted in old American music, has influenced generations of musicians. The complete sessions appeared in the Bootleg Series in 2014.
