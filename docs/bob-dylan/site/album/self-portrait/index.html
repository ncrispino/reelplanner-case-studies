Basement and Country · 1967–1970

Listen to the album

Why it matters

A 1970 double album made mostly of covers, traditional songs and a few live tracks, it baffled critics and fans expecting new Dylan songs. Later reissues of its sessions earned it a kinder reassessment.

Self Portrait was assembled from sessions in Nashville and New York in 1969 and 1970, produced by Bob Johnston, along with a handful of performances from the Isle of Wight Festival in 1969, where Dylan played with the Band. It is a sprawling double album of folk standards, pop and country covers, a few instrumentals and only a handful of originals. He sings songs by Paul Simon and Gordon Lightfoot, old ballads and pop standards, often in his Nashville croon, and some tracks carry orchestral strings and backing singers added later. Many critics took it as careless or even a deliberate joke, and Greil Marcus's long review in Rolling Stone became famous for its scorn. Dylan later suggested he had partly wanted to shake off his image as a generational prophet. In 2013 the Bootleg Series released the sessions in plainer, unadorned form as Another Self Portrait, and many listeners heard the music with fresh ears.
