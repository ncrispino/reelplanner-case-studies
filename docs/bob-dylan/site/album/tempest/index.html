Tempest

Late Renaissance · 1997–2012

Listen to the album

Why it matters

Released in the fiftieth anniversary year of his first album, it is one of his darkest records, full of violent ballads and blues. Its long title song about the Titanic and its farewell to John Lennon stand out.

Tempest was recorded in 2012 at Jackson Browne's studio in Santa Monica with Dylan's touring band and David Hidalgo, and Dylan again produced it as Jack Frost. It opens gently with "Duquesne Whistle", a jaunty swing number written with Robert Hunter, but much of what follows is dark: slow, grinding blues and long story-ballads in which betrayal and violence pile up. The title song runs about fourteen minutes and tells of the sinking of the Titanic in many verses over a lilting waltz, mixing historical figures with invented ones. The album closes with "Roll on John", a tribute to John Lennon built from echoes of Beatles songs. Dylan's voice is at its harshest here, and he uses it to growl and to tease. Critics praised its ambition and its storytelling, and it came out in the fiftieth anniversary year of his first album, which many reviews noted.
