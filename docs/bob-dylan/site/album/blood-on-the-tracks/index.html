Blood on the Tracks · 1973–1978

Listen to the album

Why it matters

Written as his marriage was breaking down, these songs of love, regret and memory are often ranked among the best of his career. He recorded it in New York in September 1974, then re-cut five songs with local musicians in Minneapolis that December, and those versions made the final record.

By 1974 Dylan's marriage was in trouble, and the songs he wrote that year are full of loss, anger, tenderness and memory, told with shifting points of view and time that slides back and forth. He recorded them in New York in September with a small group, the arrangements often reduced to his guitar, harmonica and a bass. Just before release, his brother David persuaded him to try some songs again in Minneapolis, where local musicians cut new versions of five of them over the holidays. Those fuller, livelier takes replaced the New York ones, giving the album its mix of intimacy and drive. It came out in January 1975 on Columbia, to which he had returned, and it was soon hailed as a masterpiece and a return to form. Dylan has played down its autobiographical side, but it remains one of the most admired breakup albums ever made.
