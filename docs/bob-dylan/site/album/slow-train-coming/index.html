Why it matters

His first album after becoming a born-again Christian, it set evangelical lyrics to a polished R&B sound cut at Muscle Shoals with producers Jerry Wexler and Barry Beckett. Mark Knopfler's guitar and a Grammy-winning single made it a commercial success even as it divided longtime fans.

Late in 1978 Dylan became a born-again Christian, and the songs he wrote in the following months were about faith, sin and judgment. To record them he turned to Jerry Wexler, the veteran producer of Aretha Franklin, who brought him to Muscle Shoals in Alabama in the spring of 1979 with Barry Beckett as co-producer. The band included Mark Knopfler and drummer Pick Withers of Dire Straits, along with the Muscle Shoals horns and backing singers. The result is the most polished-sounding record he had made: tight grooves, clean guitar and a confident, often fierce vocal. Released that August, it sold well, and "Gotta Serve Somebody" brought Dylan his first Grammy for a solo vocal performance. Critics and fans were split, some admiring the music and others put off by its preaching, but it opened his most controversial chapter in earnest.
