Bob Dylan

Greenwich Village · 1961–1964

Listen to the album

Why it matters

His debut, produced by John Hammond and cut in a handful of sessions in November 1961, is mostly folk and blues standards with just two originals. It sold poorly but caught the raw energy of his early club sets.

Dylan was twenty when he walked into Columbia's New York studio in late 1961, a newcomer signed by John Hammond after a few months on the Greenwich Village club circuit. The sessions were quick and cheap: just Dylan, his acoustic guitar and his harmonica, with almost no retakes. The songs came mostly from the blues and folk repertoire he had been playing nightly, and he leans hard on death and hard luck, singing in a rasping, deliberately aged voice far older than his years. Only "Talkin' New York" and "Song to Woody", his tribute to Woody Guthrie, were his own. When the album came out in 1962 it made little impression, and some at Columbia took to calling him Hammond's folly. Yet it is a vivid snapshot of a young performer still finding himself, and within months he was writing songs that would make covers unnecessary.
