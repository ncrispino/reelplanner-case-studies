The Freewheelin' Bob Dylan

Greenwich Village · 1961–1964

Listen to the album

Why it matters

His second album was made almost entirely of his own songs, and it turned a promising folk interpreter into the most talked-about songwriter of his generation. It holds protest anthems, apocalyptic visions and tender love songs side by side, under the famous cover photo of Dylan and Suze Rotolo on a snowy Greenwich Village street.

Dylan recorded his second album over many sessions between the spring of 1962 and the spring of 1963, first with John Hammond and then with Tom Wilson producing, and the running order changed more than once before release. Most of it is just Dylan alone with guitar and harmonica, though "Corrina, Corrina" carries a small backing band. The songs move freely between moods: furious protest, long visionary ballads, wry talking blues and gentle, rueful love songs shaped by his relationship with Suze Rotolo, who was away in Italy for much of the time. A song mocking the John Birch Society was dropped before release, reportedly over the label's legal worries. When Peter, Paul and Mary turned "Blowin' in the Wind" into a hit in 1963, Dylan's name spread far beyond the Village, and the album became a touchstone for the folk revival and for countless songwriters who followed.
