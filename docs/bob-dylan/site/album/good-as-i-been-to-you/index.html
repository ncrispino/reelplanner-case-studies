Good as I Been to You

Back to the Roots · 1992–1993

Listen to the album

Why it matters

His first album of just voice, guitar and harmonica since 1964, made up entirely of traditional songs and old blues.

In 1992 Dylan began an album with a band and the guitarist David Bromberg, then set those sessions aside and recorded alone in his garage studio in Malibu. What came out was Good as I Been to You, thirteen old songs played on acoustic guitar and harmonica, with no originals at all. The material ranges across British and Irish ballads such as "Arthur McBride", American murder ballads and blues, and children's songs like "Froggie Went a Courtin'". His voice is hoarse and his guitar playing busy and inventive, giving the old songs a lived-in, unpolished feel very different from the folk revival recordings of thirty years earlier. Many listeners were surprised, but critics welcomed it as a return to his roots and a sign of renewed purpose. It began a period in which old songs fed his playing and writing, and it set up the darker companion album that followed a year later.
