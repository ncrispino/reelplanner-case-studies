Blood on the Tracks · 1973–1978

Why it matters

Columbia assembled this album of cover versions from old outtakes after Dylan left the label for Asylum, without his involvement.

In 1973 Dylan's Columbia contract ran out and he signed with David Geffen's Asylum label. Columbia responded by putting together this album from leftover studio performances, mainly from the Self Portrait and New Morning sessions of 1969 and 1970, and releasing it late that year without his involvement. It is made entirely of covers: an Elvis Presley ballad, songs by Joni Mitchell and Jerry Jeff Walker, Peter La Farge's ballad about Ira Hayes, and several traditional folk songs. The performances lean on his Nashville-era croon and on backing singers, and many sound like warm-ups never meant for release. Critics widely regarded it as a low point and as a label's revenge on a departing star. Dylan returned to Columbia in 1974, and the album was long left out of print in many markets. Its tracks were eventually reissued as part of a large collection of his complete albums.
