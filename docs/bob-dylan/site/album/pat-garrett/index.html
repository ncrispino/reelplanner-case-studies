Pat Garrett & Billy the Kid

Blood on the Tracks · 1973–1978

Listen to the album

Why it matters

His soundtrack for Sam Peckinpah's western is mostly instrumental, but it produced one of his most widely covered songs.

Dylan came to Sam Peckinpah's western through the screenwriter Rudy Wurlitzer, and he ended up not only writing the score but acting in the film, as a quiet drifter named Alias who rides with Billy the Kid. The music was recorded in Mexico City, where the film was shot, and later in California, with musicians including Roger McGuinn, Jim Keltner and Booker T. Jones. Much of the album is instrumental: loose guitar figures, mournful harmonica and themes that repeat in different shades, made to sit beneath the action. The film was cut by its studio against Peckinpah's wishes, and the soundtrack drew mixed reviews at the time. But one short song, "Knockin' on Heaven's Door", written for a death scene, became a hit and went on to be recorded by Eric Clapton, Guns N' Roses and many more. It ranks among the most covered songs he has ever written.
