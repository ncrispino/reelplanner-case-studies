Shot of Love

Gospel · 1979–1981

Listen to the album

Why it matters

Mixing faith songs with secular ones, it closed the gospel period and ends with Every Grain of Sand.

Dylan recorded Shot of Love in Los Angeles in 1981, mostly with producer Chuck Plotkin, keeping the gospel singers and road musicians who had backed him on his recent tours. The sound is rougher and more rock-driven than the two records before it, with loud drums, ringing guitars and a choir of women's voices answering his lead. Alongside songs of belief sit things he had not done in two years: a tender tribute in "Lenny Bruce" to the comedian, and love songs such as "Heart of Mine". The closer, "Every Grain of Sand", is a quiet, searching hymn that many now count among his finest songs. At the time, though, reviews were cool and sales were weak, and some strong songs from the sessions were left off. Its reputation has grown since, and listeners now hear it as a bridge out of the gospel years.
